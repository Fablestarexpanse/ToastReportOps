# Quantization, uncensored fine-tunes, and Ollama runtime for a small local LLM in a factual, safety-sensitive tool (as of 2026-10-31)

Research caveats: about 15 tool calls. Many hits were aggregators, blogs, Reddit mirrors, or calculator sites; these are flagged. Vendor/uploader claims are labelled as such. Items I could not source are in Gaps.

## (1) Uncensored / abliterated / "Heretic" fine-tunes: effect on faithfulness and instruction following; the DavidAU model; supply-chain trust

### Takeaway
Heretic is automated abliteration (refusal-direction removal by weight orthogonalisation, tuned to minimise refusals and KL divergence), not a faithfulness-improving method. The DavidAU model is a multi-stage merge/fine-tune on top of a Heretic pass, with author-run-only benchmarks and an undefined "MAX". Nothing found shows an extraction/drafting tool needs it, and it adds unverified-provenance and weakened-safety risk; the plain official Qwen3.5-9B is the defensible default.

### Cited Findings
- What Heretic does: computes a per-layer "refusal direction" as difference of means of first-token residuals on "harmful" vs "harmless" prompts, orthogonalises attention out-projection and MLP down-projection matrices against it, and uses an Optuna TPE optimiser to co-minimise refusals and KL divergence from the original. — [p-e-w/heretic README](https://github.com/p-e-w/heretic)
- Heretic's own comparison (gemma-3-12b-it, vendor/author-run, RTX 5090, noted as platform-dependent): original 97/100 refusals; mlabonne abliterated 3/100 at KL 1.04; huihui-ai abliterated 3/100 at KL 0.45; Heretic 3/100 at KL 0.16. The README itself says automated metrics "never tell the whole story". Licence AGPL-3.0. Supports some hybrids such as Qwen3.5. — [p-e-w/heretic README](https://github.com/p-e-w/heretic)
- A search summary says a KL above ~1.0 generally signals serious capability damage (secondary summary, not a primary source). — [WebSearch summary citing Heretic coverage](https://www.mintlify.com/p-e-w/heretic)
- Independent literature (found via search; I read only the search-engine summaries, not the full papers): one arXiv appendix reports MMLU, HellaSwag, IFEval moving by at most ~1 point after ablation, with TruthfulQA the clear regression (-7.1) — [arXiv 2512.13655](https://www.arxiv.org/pdf/2512.13655) (attribution of the numbers to this specific paper is from a search summary; verify before quoting). A paper on reasoning models reports ablated models doing worse on benign tasks, typically 5-30% relative degradation, attributed to entanglement of the refusal direction with general capability — [Where Do Reasoning Models Refuse? arXiv 2507.03167](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.03167) (same caveat). Another comparison reports single-pass methods preserve capability better, and a further paper says traditional abliteration often degrades unrelated tasks — [arXiv 2512.18901](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.18901.pdf).
- A community write-up argues abliteration cost depends largely on how the direction is chosen (sloppy implementation) — [LessWrong post](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/7Ggt9adLgFAxWMzZP/i-bet-abliteration-s-cost-was-sloppy-implementation-i-was) (opinion).
- UGI Leaderboard measures willingness/uncensoredness, writing, and an intelligence component (NatInt); commentary advises using it as a refusal filter, not a universal intelligence ranking, and notes removing refusal does not add intelligence. I did not find a head-to-head NatInt comparison of this model vs its parent. — [UGI Leaderboard app](https://huggingface.co/spaces/DontPlanToEnd/UGI-Leaderboard/blob/main/app.py); [discussion "Intelligent vs Uncensored"](https://huggingface.co/spaces/DontPlanToEnd/UGI-Leaderboard/discussions/38)
- DavidAU model card (uploader claims): multi-stage, multi-model fine-tune and merge by DavidAU and Nightmedia, "Heretic'd" then trained further; base listed as Qwen/Qwen3.5-9B, Apache-2.0; reports KL 0.0793 vs original and 6/100 refusals vs 100/100; "NEO IMATRIX" = all quants use an importance matrix, claimed 2-4% accuracy improvement, output tensor at 16-bit; "MTP" = multi-token-prediction tensors kept at Q8_0, claimed faster with no quality difference; "MAX" is never defined on the card. — [DavidAU model card](https://huggingface.co/DavidAU/Qwen3.5-9B-The-Defiant-Fable-Uncensored-Heretic-NEO-IMATRIX-MAX-MTP-GGUF) (I read only the first ~100k of ~127k characters).
- Card benchmarks (ARC-C/E, BoolQ, HellaSwag, OpenBookQA, PIQA, Winogrande) are author-run; ARC-C 0.649 bf16 vs 0.571 for non-heretic Qwen3.5-9B-Instruct; the headline "exceeds 7 of 7" claim is not supported by the card's own table (a Qwen3.6-27B row beats it on four tasks); the card mixes version names (3.6, 3.8) and says thinking mode is better without showing results. No IFEval, hallucination, calibration or number-faithfulness results. Card advises dropping to regular quants if MTP acceptance is under 50%. — [DavidAU model card](https://huggingface.co/DavidAU/Qwen3.5-9B-The-Defiant-Fable-Uncensored-Heretic-NEO-IMATRIX-MAX-MTP-GGUF)
- The card's recommended instruct sampling: temp 0.7, top_p 0.8, top_k 20, presence penalty 1.5 (matches Qwen's own non-thinking settings) — [DavidAU card](https://huggingface.co/DavidAU/Qwen3.5-9B-The-Defiant-Fable-Uncensored-Heretic-NEO-IMATRIX-MAX-MTP-GGUF); [Qwen3.5-9B card](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen3.5-9B)
- Supply chain: Pillar Security showed GGUF chat templates (executable Jinja2) can carry conditional backdoors while weights are untouched; poisoned GGUFs reportedly passed Hugging Face's automated scans; the hub can display a clean template while the file carries a modified one. Suggested mitigations: diff the embedded template against the official one, inspect the template field in the file itself, treat provenance/signing as baseline. — [Pillar Security](https://www.pillar.security/blog/llm-backdoors-at-the-inference-level-the-threat-of-poisoned-templates); [Pillar large-scale validation](https://www.pillar.security/blog/from-discovery-to-large-scale-validation-chat-template-backdoors-across-18-models-and-4-engines); [arXiv 2602.04653](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.04653)

### Inferences
- The card's 6/100 refusals vs KL 0.0793 is only a refusal/KL measure; it says nothing about extraction fidelity. Subsequent fine-tuning and merging on top of Heretic means KL-vs-original is the only link to the official model, and any faithfulness change is unmeasured.
- A tool that extracts and drafts from supplied documents has no need for refusal removal; the downside (weaker guardrails against prompt injection in ingested documents, plus uncertain provenance) outweighs any benefit. Any "benefit" in the card's ARC-C comparison is author-run and confounded by the extra fine-tuning and comparison against a different (Instruct) baseline.
- Because the GGUF is pulled via `hf.co/` from an individual, the embedded chat template and metadata are the specific thing to audit; pin by digest/commit and diff the template against the official one if it is ever used.
- Official Qwen3.5-9B also gives a clean baseline for A/B testing on the tool's own golden set; any claim of an uncensored model being better should be shown on that set.

### Gaps
- No independent evaluation (IFEval, hallucination, calibration) of this DavidAU model or of Heretic'd Qwen3.5-9B found.
- Could not confirm the UGI scores of this model or its parent.
- Did not read the full abliteration papers; numbers above are search-summary level.
- "MAX" undefined; card like/download counts unverified.
- Safetensors vs pickle vs GGUF risk comparison not covered by sources found (GGUF has no pickle, but template risk above applies).

## (2) Quantization quality vs VRAM

### Takeaway
Measured KL data show Q8_0 and Q6_K are near-lossless, Q5_K_M modest loss, Q4_K_M noticeably more, and small models suffer more. No rigorous published benchmark of JSON or number-faithfulness by quant was found, so Q6_K or Q8_0 is the conservative choice for a number-sensitive tool and the tool's own golden set should decide.

### Cited Findings
- Qwen3.6-27B single-run mean KLD vs BF16: Q8_0 ~0.0205, Q6_K ~0.0278, Q5_K_M ~0.0528, a 4-bit Unsloth dynamic quant ~0.0666 (single run, community post via search summary; a 27B model, not 4-14B). — [search summary of Qwen3.6-27B quant benchmark](https://huy-rocks.vercel.app/everyday/05-29-2026-ai-qwen3-6-27b-quantization-benchmark)
- Qwen3-14B perplexity comparison: Q8_0 +0.04% (EN) / +0.05% (FR) vs FP16 (smaller source). — [bestllmfor guide](https://bestllmfor.com/guide/quantization-gguf-q5km-comparatif/)
- Older Mistral-7B-era llama.cpp measurements: mean KL roughly Q8_0 ~0.06, Q6_K ~0.1, Q5_K_M ~0.3, Q4_K_M ~1.0 (units/scale differ from the above; not comparable) — [llama.cpp PR #4739](https://github.com/ggerganov/llama.cpp/pull/4739).
- Unsloth Dynamic 2.0 (vendor) Gemma 3 table: Q4_K_XL KLD 0.0249 (15.4 GB), Q3_K_XL 0.088, Q2_K_XL 0.23; Gemma 3 12B Q4_0 QAT 67.07% vs BF16 67.15% MMLU. Vendor says imatrix calibrated on Wikipedia-style text overfits and text-only calibration is poor for instruct models; its comparison claims are self-reported; MMLU is hard to replicate. — [Unsloth docs](https://unsloth.ai/docs/basics/unsloth-dynamic-2.0-ggufs)
- A fine-tuned small-model classification study: Q4_K_M 0.89 accuracy vs 0.99 for Q5_K_M and FP16; Q3_K_M 0.60 (classification, not JSON). — [arXiv 2510.21970](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.21970)
- Anecdotal: Q4_K_M cut nested-schema JSON validity from ~98% to ~89% on one model (Reddit); a commenter notes schema validators miss changed argument values. Blog claims Q4_K_M hurts small models more. — [search summary of Reddit/blog results](https://bestllmfor.com/guides/quantization-q4-vs-q5-vs-q8-quality/) (unverified).
- Qwen3.5-9B architecture: 32 layers, alternating Gated DeltaNet and Gated Attention blocks; the attention blocks have 4 KV heads with head dim 256; 262,144 native context; built-in MTP. — [Qwen3.5-9B card](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen3.5-9B)
- Ollama KV cache default f16; q8_0 ~half, q4_0 ~quarter memory; applies only with flash attention; global setting. — [Ollama FAQ](https://docs.ollama.com/faq)

### Inferences
- Rough weight sizes (my arithmetic from typical bits-per-weight, not from a source): 9B at Q4_K_M about 5.5 GB, Q5_K_M about 6.5 GB, Q6_K about 7.5 GB, Q8_0 about 9.5 GB. Q6_K fits comfortably in 12 GB, and Q8_0 fits in 12 GB with limited context headroom. The output tensor at 16-bit (card claim) adds some size.
- KV cache for Qwen3.5-9B is small relative to a dense model because only the Gated Attention layers hold a KV cache. If one in four of the 32 layers is full attention (the card text I retrieved does not state the ratio, so this is assumed), f16 KV is about 4 KB per attention layer per token, roughly 32 KB/token total, i.e. about 0.25 GB at 8k and 0.5 GB at 16k, plus recurrent state. Verify with Ollama's logged memory estimate rather than trusting this.
- Hence on 12 GB, a 9B at Q6_K with 16k context is feasible; 14B dense at Q4_K_M-Q5_K_M with 8-16k is the practical limit; 24 GB allows 14B at Q8_0 or Q6_K with generous context, or 30B-A3B at ~Q4.
- Q4_K_M is a risk for number-faithful extraction, and Q5_K_M-Q6_K is where measured loss becomes small; Q8_0 buys little over Q6_K for the extra VRAM.

### Gaps
- No 4B-14B KL table by quant for Qwen3.5 found; no controlled JSON/number-faithfulness benchmark by quant; no verified VRAM-at-8k/16k measurements (only my estimates).
- Unsloth Qwen3.5 GGUF benchmark page surfaced but not read.
- The "NEO IMATRIX +2-4%" claim is unverified; imatrix quality depends on calibration data.

## (3) Ollama runtime behaviour, MTP, and hardware sizing

### Takeaway
Use the API `think: false` (not `/no_think`, which Qwen3.5 does not support) together with `format`, and upgrade to a version containing the fix for the format-ignored-when-thinking-off bug (reported fixed in 0.31.2). Set `num_ctx` explicitly (default 4096 is a silent truncation risk). MTP is a llama.cpp feature requiring explicit flags; I found no evidence Ollama uses it, so treat it as ignored for the "MTP" GGUF.

### Cited Findings
- Qwen3.5 thinking is on by default; disable via chat-template `enable_thinking: False`; the model card states "Qwen3.5 does not officially support the soft switch of Qwen3, i.e., /think and /nothink". — [Qwen3.5-9B card](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen3.5-9B)
- Ollama `think` API parameter: true / false / null (model default) / string level; check `/api/show` for the `thinking` metadata; if absent the model may still think by its own behaviour; reasoning returns in `message.thinking`. — [Ollama thinking docs](https://docs.ollama.com/capabilities/thinking)
- Bug: `format` (JSON/schema) ignored for Qwen3.5 when thinking is disabled (the chat template inserts an empty think block, so Ollama never sees the end-of-thinking token). Closed as completed via PR #15901 on 2026-07-07; reporter confirmed fixed in Ollama 0.31.2 (2026-07-09). Reports: still ignored on `/api/chat` with a `/no_think` system prompt in 0.30.7 (pre-fix); `/api/generate` reportedly enforced format; custom model without the thinking capability flag also worked; one user said it also affected Qwen3 from 0.30.0; a backport request for 0.21.1 had no reply. — [ollama/ollama#14645](https://github.com/ollama/ollama/issues/14645)
- Setting `PARAMETER think false` in a Modelfile reportedly does not work for Qwen3.5 (issue #14617, Reddit-sourced); pass `think` per request, and check that agent clients forward it. LM Studio/llama.cpp reports show similar thinking-flag problems. — [search summary of Reddit/issue results](https://github.com/ollama/ollama/issues/14645)
- Ollama FAQ: default context window 4096 tokens (override with OLLAMA_CONTEXT_LENGTH or per-request num_ctx); OLLAMA_KEEP_ALIVE default 5 minutes (request `keep_alive` overrides); OLLAMA_MAX_LOADED_MODELS default 3 per GPU; OLLAMA_NUM_PARALLEL default 1, and context scales with parallel requests (2K x 4 = 8K); for GPU inference a new model must fully fit in VRAM to load concurrently, otherwise requests queue and idle models are unloaded; flash attention is used automatically when supported (OLLAMA_FLASH_ATTENTION=1/0 to force); KV cache type via OLLAMA_KV_CACHE_TYPE, only with flash attention. — [Ollama FAQ](https://docs.ollama.com/faq). Note: third-party guides disagree on the 4096 default and mention VRAM-dependent defaults (4k under 24 GiB, 32k at 24-48 GiB) — [SSD Nodes](https://www.ssdnodes.com/learn/ollama-context-length-num-ctx); check the installed version's docs. A request-level num_ctx overrides the server default.
- MTP: llama.cpp merged MTP support in PR #22673 (2026-05-16), enabled by `--spec-type draft-mtp` and `--spec-draft-n-max`; only works if the GGUF contains the MTP head; early reports said hybrid/linear-attention models like Qwen3.5 needed an unmerged GDN partial-rollback PR (#22400; status unverified) and MTP did not yet support parallel slots. One news article claims the change propagates to Ollama automatically, but the same article says Ollama has announced no timeline; I found no Ollama issue or release note confirming MTP use. — [llama.cpp PR #22673](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/pull/22673); [aiweekly summary](https://aiweekly.co/alerts/llamacpp-adds-multi-token-prediction-for-faster-local-ai); [LocalAI/Reddit summaries via search]
- The DavidAU card's MTP speed figures (about 130 tok/s regular vs 185+ with MTP on an RTX 5090 in LM Studio at Q4_K_S) are author-run and in LM Studio, not Ollama. — [DavidAU card](https://huggingface.co/DavidAU/Qwen3.5-9B-The-Defiant-Fable-Uncensored-Heretic-NEO-IMATRIX-MAX-MTP-GGUF)
- Throughput (all estimates, not measurements): calculator site estimates Qwen3.5-9B Q4_K_M on RTX 3060 12GB at ~40-43 tok/s; Qwen3-30B-A3B Q4_K_M on RTX 4090 ~83 tok/s in one table and ~116 in another from the same site; 30B-A3B on a 3060 12GB ~7 tok/s (too big, offload). A community report puts Qwen3.5-9B Q4_K_M on a 6 GB 3060 at 15-22 tok/s. — [willitrunai 3060](https://willitrunai.com/zh/can-run/qwen-3.5-9b-on-rtx-3060-12gb); [willitrunai 4090 30B-A3B](https://willitrunai.com/ja/can-run/qwen-3-30b-a3b-on-rtx-4090-24gb); [markaicode](https://markaicode.com/benchmarks/lm-studio-qwen-benchmark/)

### Inferences
- Safest configuration for a structured tool: Ollama at or above the fixed version; `think: false` on each request; `format` set to a JSON schema; also validate output in code, because a schema check does not catch changed numeric values; `num_ctx` set explicitly at 8192-16384 and prompt token counts compared against `prompt_eval_count` to detect truncation; temperature low (the vendor's non-thinking 0.7 is a chat default, so tune on the golden set); `keep_alive` long enough (or -1) for an interactive tool to avoid reload latency.
- Chat plus embedding on one GPU: the default MAX_LOADED_MODELS of 3 is not the binding limit; VRAM is. A 9B Q6_K (~7.5 GB) plus a small embedding model fits in 12 GB, but each model's num_ctx and parallel slots add memory; keep NUM_PARALLEL=1 unless needed. Verify with `ollama ps`.
- Because MTP requires explicit llama.cpp flags and head support, and Ollama documentation does not mention it, assume the MTP GGUF runs as a normal model in Ollama; the MTP tensors cost a little disk/VRAM and give no benefit. A plain (non-MTP) quant from a trusted source is simpler.
- MoE (30B-A3B) gives dense-9B-class speed with larger-model knowledge only if the whole model fits (~18-19 GB at Q4), so it suits 24 GB cards and not 12 GB; for 12 GB, a 9B-14B dense model is the better trade. Extraction fidelity of 3B-active models vs dense 9B for this workload was not found measured.

### Gaps
- No measured (non-calculator) tok/s for 4B/9B/14B/30B-A3B on a 4090 or 3060/4070; the 4090 9B figure absent.
- Official Ollama release notes for MTP, and the exact first Ollama version supporting qwen3.5, not checked; whether #22400 merged unverified.
- Other 2026 qwen3.5 Ollama regressions beyond #14645 (e.g. #14617, #14850, #15540) only seen as titles in search; not read.
- Whether `hf.co/` pulls in Ollama apply the embedded GGUF chat template vs the repo's, and whether `think` capability metadata is present for the community fine-tune, not verified.
