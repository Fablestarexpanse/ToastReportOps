# Test official Qwen, Gemma, Granite before trusting Heretic

**The evidence does not justify keeping the community "Heretic" fine-tune of Qwen3.5-9B as the default, and it does not yet justify any specific replacement either; the right move is a short bake-off on your own data, starting with a plain official model.** The Heretic method removes refusals and does nothing for faithfulness, and the specific DavidAU build adds extra training, undefined branding ("MAX") and only author-run benchmarks that never test extraction, calibration or number fidelity ([Heretic README](https://github.com/p-e-w/heretic), [DavidAU card](https://huggingface.co/DavidAU/Qwen3.5-9B-The-Defiant-Fable-Uncensored-Heretic-NEO-IMATRIX-MAX-MTP-GGUF)). No independent benchmark of any candidate on your actual tasks (number-faithful extraction, calibrated judgment, cited drafting) was found; the only per-model small-model faithfulness table is a grounded-summarisation proxy ([Vectara](https://github.com/vectara/hallucination-leaderboard)). Provisional first-to-test order: on 12 GB, official `qwen3.5:9b` (the true baseline), `gemma4:12b` (QAT) and `granite4.2:8b`; on 24 GB, `gemma4:26b` (QAT, MoE), `granite4.2:30b`, `qwen3.8:27b` and `gemma4:31b`. That order is my judgment from thin evidence (a proxy table, model-card features, size fit), not a measured ranking. Two code-level problems need fixing regardless of model: the repository's `/no_think` switch does nothing on Qwen3.5, and swapping the embedding model forces a full re-embed. Embeddings: keep `nomic-embed-text` until a 3-way bake-off on labelled snippet pairs says otherwise; `embeddinggemma-2` (270m or 440m) is the lowest-friction challenger because it is natively 768-dimensional. `qev-0.8b` is a plausible but entirely vendor-evidenced judgment helper to test, not adopt.

## What to do first

Do these in order; the first three cost little and remove known defects. First, check your Ollama version: the notes record that `format` (JSON schema) was ignored on Qwen3.5 when thinking was off, fixed in Ollama 0.31.2, and your own `.env.example` says Qwen3.5 needs 0.32 or later ([ollama#14645](https://github.com/ollama/ollama/issues/14645)). Second, stop relying on `/no_think`. Qwen's own card says Qwen3.5 "does not officially support the soft switch" ([Qwen3.5-9B card](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen3.5-9B)), yet `backend/src/toast/llm/client.py` prepends `/no_think` to every extraction prompt and `core/config.py` documents it as the suppression mechanism. The practical consequence (inference from the notes plus the code) is that extraction on the current default is probably thinking, which costs latency and, per the little evidence available, may not help faithfulness. The fix is to send a per-request `think: false` ([Ollama thinking docs](https://docs.ollama.com/capabilities/thinking)). Whether Ollama's OpenAI-compatible `/v1/chat/completions` route (the one `chat_json` uses) forwards that field was not established by the notes; test it, and fall back to the native `/api/chat` if not. A Modelfile `PARAMETER think false` was reported not to work for Qwen3.5, so do not rely on that either. Third, set `num_ctx` explicitly (8192 to 16384); the Ollama default is 4096 per its FAQ, though third-party guides disagree, and silent truncation would corrupt extraction ([Ollama FAQ](https://docs.ollama.com/faq)). Fourth, run the bake-off in the test plan below. Fifth, hold off on the embedding migration until the labelled-pair test exists.

## The default model: what was found about the DavidAU Heretic build, and what was not

The finding should be stated fairly in both directions. What Heretic does is documented and not sinister: it computes a per-layer "refusal direction" and orthogonalises weights against it, using an optimiser to minimise refusals and KL divergence from the original ([Heretic README](https://github.com/p-e-w/heretic)). Its own comparison, run by the author on Gemma 3 12B, shows 3/100 refusals at KL 0.16 versus 97/100 for the original. The DavidAU card (uploader claims) lists base Qwen3.5-9B, Apache-2.0, KL 0.0793 and 6/100 refusals, and describes a multi-stage fine-tune and merge on top of the Heretic pass ([DavidAU card](https://huggingface.co/DavidAU/Qwen3.5-9B-The-Defiant-Fable-Uncensored-Heretic-NEO-IMATRIX-MAX-MTP-GGUF)). Beyond that, the researcher found several problems with what the card shows. Its benchmarks (ARC, BoolQ, HellaSwag and similar) are author-run; its headline "exceeds 7 of 7" claim is contradicted by its own table, where a Qwen3.6-27B row wins on four tasks; the ARC-C comparison is against the non-Heretic Instruct model and is confounded by the extra fine-tuning; the card mixes version names (3.6, 3.8); "MAX" is never defined; and the "NEO IMATRIX +2-4% accuracy" and "MTP faster" claims are unverified and measured in LM Studio, not Ollama. There are no IFEval, hallucination, calibration or number-faithfulness results for it, and none were found independently.

What was not found is any evidence of malice. The supply-chain concern is generic but real: Pillar Security showed GGUF chat templates are executable Jinja2 and can carry conditional backdoors while the weights stay untouched, with poisoned files reportedly passing Hugging Face's automated scans ([Pillar Security](https://www.pillar.security/blog/llm-backdoors-at-the-inference-level-the-threat-of-poisoned-templates), [arXiv 2602.04653](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.04653)). Nobody reported a problem with this particular file, and the notes did not inspect its template; whether Ollama applies the embedded template for `hf.co/` pulls was also unverified. Independent literature on abliteration is mixed and was read only at search-summary level: one source reports MMLU, HellaSwag and IFEval moving at most about one point with TruthfulQA down 7.1 ([arXiv 2512.13655](https://www.arxiv.org/pdf/2512.13655)), another reports 5 to 30% relative degradation on benign tasks for reasoning models ([arXiv 2507.03167](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.03167)). Calling it "worse" is therefore not supported; calling it "unproven and unnecessary" is. The researcher's inference, which I adopt, is that a tool that extracts and drafts from supplied documents does not need refusal removal, and a refusal-weakened model is a worse fit for ingesting untrusted web text. The repo's own stack mitigates injection structurally, per `DECISIONS.md`, but that was tested on qwen3, not this model. If you keep the model, diff its embedded chat template against the official Qwen3.5 one and pin the file by digest.

## Per-task rankings

**Task 1, faithful structured extraction.** The only comparable evidence is Vectara's grounded-summarisation leaderboard (HHEM-2.3 judge, updated 2026-10-22), which is a proxy, not extraction. Hallucination rate (answer rate): Gemma-3-12b-it **4.4%** (97.4%), Qwen3-8B **4.8%** (99.9%), Gemma-4-26b-a4b-it **5.2%**, Granite-4.0-h-small **5.2%** (100%), Qwen3-14B 5.4%, Gemma-4-31b-it 7.4%, Nemotron-3-Nano-30B-A3B 9.6%, then the newer generations that look worse: Qwen3.5-35b-a3b 10.5%, Qwen3.5-27b **12.1%**, Ministral-3-14b/8b/3b 19.4/21.7/24.2% ([Vectara](https://github.com/vectara/hallucination-leaderboard)). Two cautions apply. HHEM is itself a noisy model (best detector F1-macro 55% on hard samples in FaithBench), so the researcher's reading, which I share, is that 3.7 to 7.4% results are roughly tied ([FaithBench](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.13210v1)). And Qwen3.5-9B, Qwen3.8, Granite 4.2 and Gemma 4 12B have no Vectara row in the notes at all; the closest relatives are used as stand-ins. The one worrying signal for your lineage is that the Qwen3.5 rows (27B, 35B-A3B) are materially worse than Qwen3 and Gemma on this proxy, which is the base of your current default; the 9B row was not retrieved. Provisional ranking, 12 GB: (1) `qwen3.5:9b` official, no-think, as baseline and because it is the only candidate whose integration (chat template quirks, format fix) is already known; (2) `gemma4:12b`, QAT 7.2 GB; (3) `granite4.2:8b` (5.3 GB, vendor positions it for extraction and JSON output ([Ollama granite4.2](https://ollama.com/library/granite4.2))). The ordering among these three is not evidence-based; the gold-set bake-off decides. Provisional ranking, 24 GB: (1) `gemma4:26b` QAT (16 GB, MoE with 3.8B active, leaves context headroom, 5.2% proxy); (2) `granite4.2:30b` (18 GB); (3) `qwen3.8:27b` (18 GB; Artificial Analysis gives it 10 non-reasoning versus 20 at Xhigh on a general index, which says nothing about extraction ([Artificial Analysis](https://artificialanalysis.ai/models/open-source/small))); (4) `gemma4:31b` QAT (19 GB, tight, 7.4% proxy). Avoid for this task on current evidence: Ministral-3 (worst Vectara results), Phi-4-mini, SmolLM2 and sub-4B models (high variance in a German-language benchmark seen via snippet only ([arXiv 2606.13111](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.13111))), and Nemotron-3-nano. Evidence on whether 24 GB models beat 9B ones for extraction is absent: within Qwen3 and Gemma 3, size had no consistent effect on the proxy. Use schema `format` plus the schema in the prompt, temperature 0, nullable fields, and code-side checks that every quote is an exact substring of the source and every number, date and place token occurs in it; the UNDRR disaster-extraction paper found quote-verbatim rates ranging 47% to 95% across frontier models and stressed that structural compliance "does not establish whether an extracted statement is supported" ([arXiv 2609.23853](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.23853v1)). Treat that verification design as the researcher's inference, untested.

**Task 2, yes/no and pick-one judgment with calibrated confidence.** Use the Task 1 winner (or a second model from the same pool), with thinking tested on and off, and read P(yes) from logprobs rather than asking for a verbalised confidence. RLHF-tuned models are reported to be over-confident when verbalising ([arXiv 2410.09724](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2410.09724v1)), and the standard fix is post-hoc Platt or isotonic recalibration on a few hundred labelled items. Ollama's API gained logprobs in v0.12.11 per release notes, but a third-party page says otherwise and the repo's client does not use logprobs today; test it on your pinned version ([release notes mirror](https://newreleases.io/project/github/ollama/ollama/release/v0.12.11-rc1)). Supply dates as explicit inputs and compute date arithmetic in code, since models give outdated answers about time ([DyKnow](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.08700v1)), and include a "none / new incident" option in pick-one questions. Whether reasoning helps here is open: summarisation research found more reasoning lowering faithfulness ([arXiv 2512.03503](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.03503), proprietary models), while the general index above doubles with reasoning. Rank: (1) your best general instruct model with calibrated logprob; (2) `labqoat/qev-0.8b` as a challenger and cheap first stage; (3) a fine-tuned encoder or SetFit classifier for the "is this about a California wildfire at all" filter. The qev-0.8b evidence is entirely vendor-run: a Qwen3.5-0.8B-Base fine-tune, Apache-2.0, returning probabilities for yes/no, pick-one (up to 255 options) and ordinal questions in one forward pass, with a self-reported 77.1% on "Benchmark Heaven" (231 items, own harness) and ECE 0.063 on the authors' own calibration split ([HF card](https://huggingface.co/labqoat/qev-0.8b), [GGUF card](https://huggingface.co/labqoat/qev-0.8b-GGUF)). The company publishes no team, terms or pricing, the repo had 545 monthly downloads and one unanswered training-data question, input length is undisclosed, and the card says it rarely picks "cannot answer" and advises recalibration on your data. A separately named AustinFu/Qev-0.8B reports a lower 73.16% on the same 231-item set, with no stated relationship. It runs in Ollama only via a custom `/v1/systemone` endpoint (`ollama pull hf.co/labqoat/qev-0.8b-GGUF:Q8_0`, tested on Ollama 0.40.4 per the card), so adopting it means writing a new client path, not changing a model name.

**Task 3, drafting short public updates from numbered facts.** No benchmark of this task was found. The model matters less than the harness: have the model emit sentences tagged with fact IDs, copy evacuation text into the output by code substitution rather than model re-typing, reject any number not present in the cited facts, and keep human review on orders (researcher's inference, untested). Ranking follows Task 1 (same pool, thinking off, temperature low), with the practical note that the Gemma 4 and Granite 4.2 families are the newest Apache-2.0 options and that Vectara's average summary lengths vary widely (67 to 120 words), so check length compliance too.

**Task 5, optional fast triage classifier.** Prefer a fine-tuned encoder: DeBERTa-v3 is the more sample-efficient base, ModernBERT is faster with 8k context, and SetFit trains from few examples ([arXiv 2504.08716](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.08716v2), [SetFit](https://huggingface.co/blog/setfit)). The researcher's inference, which is plausible but unmeasured here, is that a few hundred labels suffice for a topic filter and that such a classifier is easier to calibrate than an LLM. `qev-0.8b` (Q8_0 GGUF, a small model on the same GPU) is the LLM-shaped alternative, with every performance number vendor-run; if neither is wanted, `granite4.2:3b` (2.2 GB) or `qwen3.5:2b/4b` are the generic small options, but no evidence on their triage accuracy was found. A cascade (cheap stage, calibrated threshold, escalate uncertain items) is supported by recent self-reported preprints on other tasks only ([ICML 2026](https://icml.cc/virtual/2026/67782)).

The gte family and newer bge variants were not verified in the notes, and no Ollama tag was found for Microsoft harrier-oss or jina-embeddings-v5 (jina's nano GGUF repo lists CC-BY-NC-4.0, so check before commercial use); absence of a tag was not proven. The dimension question settles the migration cost. Because vec0 columns are fixed (`float[768]` in `0001_init.sql`) and vectors from different models are not comparable even at the same dimension, any model change means a new table and a full re-embed; truncating Qwen3-0.6B from 1024 to 768 via Matryoshka (`vec_slice` then `vec_normalize`) keeps the schema but not comparability ([sqlite-vec docs](https://alexgarcia.xyz/sqlite-vec/api-reference.html)). EmbeddingGemma-2 at 768 native is therefore the lowest-friction schema match, though it still requires re-embedding and re-calibrating the 0.10 and 0.45 thresholds. For event matching, embeddings alone are likely to rate "two different fires in the same county" as similar; the researcher's inference, untested, is that location, date and acreage overlap checks as a veto, plus an optional cross-encoder on the uncertain band, beat a bigger embedding model. The supporting evidence is a news-dedupe study where a cross-encoder re-rank lifted ARI from 91.5 to 93.7 over bi-encoder alone and both beat n-gram overlap at 75.0, on long historical articles rather than short snippets ([arXiv 2210.04261](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2210.04261)). Published threshold guidance (0.9 to 0.95 cosine similarity) does not transfer across models ([NewsCatcher](https://www.newscatcherapi.com/docs/news-api/guides-and-concepts/articles-deduplication.md)).

## Test plan using the repository's attribution gold set

First, understand what the gold set can and cannot do, because it is easy to misuse. `attribution_eval.py` does not call an LLM: it feeds each fact's pre-recorded `extracted_name`, `lat`, `lon` and `observed_at` to the deterministic `score_candidates` and applies the production rule (score at least 0.75 and a 0.15 gap over the runner-up). The fixture holds **20 incidents and 131 facts** captured 2026-07-20. Of those, 72 are tier-1 official records with no extracted name (attributed by reference in production, so never scored), leaving **59 scored facts** (49 tier 4, 10 tier 2), of which only 24 across the file carry coordinates. The `note` field says it is an honest but easy subset, whose numbers are an upper bound. So as-is it measures the scorer, not the model; it cannot choose a model until extraction is re-run from the `content` text. A candidate model's effect enters through what it extracts. The harness extension below is my proposal, not existing code, and the set is small, so differences of a few facts are noise.

| Step | What to run | Pass or decide rule |
|---|---|---|
| 0. Baseline | Run `python -m toast.eval.attribution_eval` unchanged and record precision, recall, wrong, missed, extractor_miss | Reference numbers; precision should be near 100% per the module's own stated standard |
| 1. Extraction replay | For each of the 59 scored facts (and the 72 tier-1 as negatives for hallucinated names), call the candidate model on `content` with the production extraction prompt, thinking off, temperature 0, schema `format`; replace `extracted_name` and `location_text` with the model's output; rerun `score_candidates` | Primary metric: **wrong attributions** (target 0), then recall; also count invented names not in `content`, and nulls where `content` names a fire (the module's `extractor_miss`) |
| 2. Verbatim check | In code, require every extracted name, number and quote to appear in `content` | Hallucination rate = failures per fact; reject any candidate that invents tokens |
| 3. Pick-one judgment | Reuse the 20-incident world: give the model the fact plus candidate incident list with a "none" option and ask it to pick; log P(choice) via logprobs | Accuracy against `truth_incident`; reliability curve; compare thinking on vs off and qev-0.8b `choice` (custom endpoint) |
| 4. Calibrate | Fit isotonic or Platt on a held-out half (stratify by incident), choose two thresholds from the precision-coverage curve | Report ECE or Brier on the other half; do not report a single accuracy |
| 5. Extend the set | Hand-label 200 to 300 items from the live ledger for new-vs-old, still-burning and no-fire-named facts, plus 100 to 200 snippet pairs (duplicate, same-incident-different-fact, different-incident-same-county) | Needed because the gold set contains none of these cases and no fact lacking a named fire |
| 6. Embedding bake-off | Embed the pair set with `nomic-embed-text`, `embeddinggemma-2:270m` and `:440m`, `qwen3-embedding:0.6b`, using correct prefixes; compare ROC and the precision at chosen thresholds, with and without a place/number overlap veto | Choose per-model thresholds before comparing; switch only if clearly better on pairs |
| 7. Operational | Per model: tokens per second, load time, peak VRAM via `ollama ps` on both 4090 and a 12 GB card; Ollama version recorded; run each 3 times | Remove any candidate that does not fit with 16k context, or whose JSON fails validation |

Candidate matrix for steps 1 to 4: 12 GB, `qwen3.5:9b` official, `gemma4:12b`, `granite4.2:8b`, and the current Heretic default as a control; 24 GB, `gemma4:26b`, `granite4.2:30b`, `qwen3.8:27b`, `gemma4:31b`. Run with Q6_K or Q8_0 where available on 9B-class models. Decision rule I suggest: choose the smallest model with zero wrong attributions and zero invented tokens on the replay, with the best calibrated pick-one accuracy; if two tie, prefer the one that fits the 12 GB card, so one config serves both builds. Treat Qwen3.5-family results with extra scrutiny given the Vectara signal.

## Verified versus unverified

Verified in the sense of read directly from primary pages by the researchers (though through a summarising fetch tool, so exact figures should be re-checked): the existence, sizes, tags and licence statements of the Ollama and Hugging Face pages cited above; the Qwen3.5 statement that `/think` and `/nothink` soft switches are unsupported; the Ollama thinking API; the closure of ollama#14645 with a fix in 0.31.2; Vectara's published rows (a vendor-run board using a noisy judge, but applied to all models); the sqlite-vec fixed-dimension column design; and the repository facts I confirmed myself (the `/no_think` prepending, `json_object` response format, fixed cosine thresholds, no logprob use, and the structure of the gold set).

Vendor-reported only, not independently reproduced: every benchmark on the Gemma 4, Qwen3.8, Qwen3-Embedding, EmbeddingGemma-2, nomic, harrier and qev-0.8b cards; the DavidAU card's claims; Heretic's KL and refusal comparison. Unverified or from search summaries: BenchLM rankings (method unknown); the abliteration paper numbers; the quantisation KL figures from a community post; throughput figures from calculator sites; the cascade and calibration preprints; the German benchmark and ACL-Verbatim figures; the claim that Ollama silently ignores schema property descriptions. Researcher inference rather than evidence: the extraction pipeline design with substring checks, the claim that thinking mode adds risk rather than value for extraction, the 24 GB context headroom estimate, the KV-cache arithmetic, the entity-overlap veto for dedupe, and the encoder-for-triage recommendation. Notable gaps where the notes disagree or are silent: no independent benchmark of any candidate on structured extraction, number fidelity, radio-transcript text or wildfire reports; no Vectara or Artificial Analysis row for Qwen3.5-9B, Granite 4.2, Gemma 4 12B or the DavidAU build; the Ollama `/v1` route's handling of `think` is unchecked; the licence terms for Qwen3.5/3.6 weights, Ornith, gpt-oss and LFM are unverified; and the unnamed newcomers (Ornith-1.5, d1, d3, nimble and others) were deliberately not recommended. The test plan above is designed to close the first two gaps with your own data.

## Conclusion

The central finding is a shift in where the risk sits. Model choice among current Apache-2.0 options in the 8B to 31B range is probably a second-order decision on this evidence, because the proxy scores of the better families cluster and no source measures your tasks. What the notes show with confidence are first-order integration defects that apply whichever model you choose: a thinking switch that Qwen3.5 ignores, a schema-enforcement bug fixed only in recent Ollama builds, unprefixed embedding calls, and hard-coded thresholds that silently break on a model swap. Fixing those, and enforcing the verbatim checks in code, will likely move faithfulness more than any model swap.

The second implication is about the gold set. At 59 scoreable facts of easy, single-sentence, name-bearing text, it can detect a model that invents or drops a fire name, which is exactly the failure that corrupts an incident ledger, but it cannot rank models that all do well, and it cannot certify the judgment, dedupe or drafting tasks. Spending a day hand-labelling a few hundred real ledger items will buy more decision quality than reading further benchmarks, and it is also the only way to decide whether the vendor-evidenced `qev-0.8b` or a newer embedding model deserves a place in the stack.
